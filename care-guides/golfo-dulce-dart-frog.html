Golfo Dulce Poison Dart Frog Care Guide

Phyllobates vittatus

Diurnal Bioactive Easy-Intermediate

Golfo Dulce poison dart frogs are small, active terrestrial frogs from southwestern Costa Rica. They do best in mature, densely planted vivariums with stable humidity, good airflow, abundant leaf litter, and a reliable supply of tiny live prey.

Water and heat matter: Dart frogs can decline quickly from overheating, poor water quality, or stagnant saturated conditions. High humidity should be paired with drainage and ventilation, and deep open water should be avoided.

Enclosure Requirements

  • A pair can be housed in an 18 x 18 x 18 inch front-opening vivarium; 24 x 18 x 18 inches or larger is preferred for a small group.
  • Use a drainage layer, screen separator, tropical bioactive substrate, thick leaf litter, cork, hides, and dense plant cover.
  • Keep water features shallow. Dart frogs are not strong swimmers and unguarded deep pools can become a drowning risk.

Temperature, Lighting & UVB

  • Daytime temperatures around 70-76 F are ideal, with a modest nighttime drop into roughly 65-72 F.
  • Avoid sustained temperatures of 80 F or higher; glass vivariums can overheat rapidly in direct sun.
  • Provide a 12-hour day/night cycle. Plant lighting is useful, and optional very-low-level UVB should only cover part of the enclosure with abundant shade.

Humidity & Substrate

  • Aim for a humid enclosure that rises after misting and then dries slightly before the next cycle rather than remaining stagnant and soaked.
  • Leaf litter should stay lightly damp underneath while some surface areas are allowed to be less wet.
  • Springtails are strongly recommended; dwarf isopods can also be used when populations are kept appropriate for small frogs.

Food & Supplements

  • Flightless fruit flies are the staple. Small juveniles do well on Drosophila melanogaster and springtails; larger frogs can also take Drosophila hydei and other tiny cultured feeders.
  • Juveniles generally need small meals daily or nearly daily; adults commonly eat several times per week depending on condition and prey size.
  • Dust feeders lightly with a quality dart-frog calcium/vitamin supplement on most feedings and use vitamin A or multivitamin products conservatively according to a sound supplement plan.

Decor, Handling & Cohabitation

  • Dense cover is important for security and natural daytime activity; provide bromeliads, cork, leaf litter, seed pods, hides, and sturdy tropical plants.
  • Avoid routine handling. Move frogs with a clean cup or smooth container instead of bare hands whenever practical.
  • Pairs and small same-species groups can work in adequately sized planted enclosures, but monitor adults for food competition, weight loss, chasing, and breeding-related conflict. Do not mix species, color forms, or localities.
