Chocolate Desert Millipede Care Guide

Orthoporus ornatus

Desert Detritivore Burrowing Beginner

Chocolate desert millipedes are large southwestern detritivores adapted to seasonally dry habitats, but they still depend on access to moisture within the soil. Good captive care combines strong ventilation and a relatively dry surface with a deeper damp refuge and abundant decomposing plant material.

Desert does not mean bone-dry: These millipedes need a moisture gradient. Keep the deeper portion of the substrate damp enough for burrowing and hydration while allowing the surface to remain noticeably drier and well ventilated.

Enclosure Requirements

  • Use a secure, well-ventilated enclosure with generous floor area and enough depth for several inches of substrate.
  • Provide a deep burrowing layer and surface cover such as cork, hardwood pieces, leaf litter, and sturdy hides.
  • Avoid placing the enclosure in direct sun or using intense heating that can rapidly desiccate the substrate.

Temperature & Moisture Gradient

  • Warm room temperatures generally suit this species; avoid extreme heat and monitor the enclosure rather than guessing from room conditions.
  • Maintain a dry-to-damp gradient: the surface can be relatively dry while lower substrate remains lightly moist and accessible.
  • Do not keep the entire enclosure saturated. Strong ventilation should prevent stagnant, swampy conditions while preserving a damp retreat below.

Substrate & Food

  • Build the substrate from pesticide-free organic soil mixed with decomposing hardwood leaves and rotten hardwood; the organic material is a major food source.
  • Keep leaf litter and edible hardwood available and replenish them as they break down.
  • Supplement with small amounts of squash, zucchini, carrot, sweet potato, cucumber, leafy vegetables, and occasional fruit; remove spoiled leftovers promptly.

Calcium, Decor & Maintenance

  • Provide a calcium source such as cuttlebone or calcium carbonate.
  • Use cork bark, hardwood, leaf litter, and other stable natural decor that provides shade and grazing surfaces.
  • Avoid frequent complete substrate changes and never disturb a buried animal that may be molting unless an emergency requires it.

Cohabitation & Handling

  • Chocolate desert millipedes can generally be kept in same-species groups when space, food, and substrate are adequate.
  • Monitor group enclosures for competition and maintain enough edible substrate for every animal.
  • Handle gently and support the body. Defensive secretions can stain and may irritate skin, eyes, or the mouth, so wash hands afterward.
