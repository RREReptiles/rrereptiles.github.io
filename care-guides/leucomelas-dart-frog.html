Bumblebee Poison Dart Frog Care Guide

Dendrobates leucomelas

Diurnal Climbing Dart Frog Beginner-Intermediate

Dendrobates leucomelas, commonly called the bumblebee or yellow-banded poison dart frog, is an active diurnal dart frog that uses both the forest floor and low vertical structure. A mature planted vivarium with leaf litter, climbing surfaces, clean airflow, and dependable tiny live feeders supports natural behavior.

Species-specific setup: Leucomelas readily use vertical space, but height should supplement—not replace—adequate floor area, leaf litter, hides, and feeding zones. Group housing requires enough space and close observation for territorial or breeding-related aggression.

Enclosure Requirements

  • An 18 x 18 x 18 inch front-opening vivarium is a practical minimum for a pair; larger enclosures are strongly preferred for groups.
  • Provide both floor cover and usable vertical structure such as cork, planted backgrounds, branches, and bromeliads.
  • Use a drainage layer and bioactive substrate topped with abundant clean leaf litter; keep deep water areas inaccessible.

Temperature, Lighting & UVB

  • Maintain generally cool tropical temperatures, roughly 70-76 F during the day with safe modest nighttime drops.
  • Avoid prolonged temperatures around 80 F and above. Never rely on direct sun or strong basking lamps for heat.
  • Use a consistent day/night cycle and plant lighting. Optional very-low-level UVB may be provided over only part of the enclosure with plentiful shade.

Humidity & Water

  • Maintain high humidity with ventilation and drainage; the enclosure should cycle after misting rather than stay waterlogged.
  • Keep leaf litter damp underneath with some less-saturated surface areas available.
  • Use amphibian-safe water for misting and any shallow dishes or features, and keep water areas clean.

Food & Supplements

  • Flightless fruit flies are the dietary staple, supplemented with springtails and other appropriately tiny cultured invertebrates.
  • Feed juveniles frequently and adults several times per week, adjusting quantities so large numbers of uneaten flies do not accumulate.
  • Dust prey with a high-quality dart-frog calcium/vitamin supplement on most feedings and manage vitamin A carefully as part of the supplementation plan.

Decor, Handling & Cohabitation

  • Use dense planting, cork, leaf litter, seed pods, climbing surfaces, and multiple retreats to reduce exposure and resource competition.
  • Avoid unnecessary handling; use a clean cup or container for transfers whenever possible.
  • Leucomelas can often be maintained in same-species groups more successfully than some dart frogs, but adults still need monitoring for chasing, food competition, weight loss, hiding, and breeding-related conflict. Never mix species, morphs, or localities.
