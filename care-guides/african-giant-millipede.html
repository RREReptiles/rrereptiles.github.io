African Giant Millipede Care Guide

Archispirostreptus gigas

Beginner to IntermediateHumid forest floorDeep burrowing substrate

Deep edible hardwood substrate is the most important part of this tropical millipede's enclosure. Maintain warm, humid soil, a drier surface refuge and gentle handling.

Species Profile

Common name: African Giant Millipede / Giant African Millipede

Scientific name: Archispirostreptus gigas

Adult size: Usually 8-12 in (20-30 cm), occasionally approaching 13 in (33 cm)

Captive lifespan: Often estimated at 5-7 years; some individuals live longer

Native range: Tropical and subtropical East Africa

Habitat and activity: Humid forest-floor detritivore; terrestrial, burrowing and often active at night

Diet: Decaying hardwood leaves and white-rotted wood; occasional vegetables and fruit

Care difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate

Enclosure and Equipment

Shopping List

Secure ventilated terrestrial enclosure, ideally at least 24 × 18 in (61 × 46 cm) of floor space for a large single adult.

Deep organic soil with decomposed pesticide-free hardwood leaves and white-rotted wood; cork bark, leaf litter, thermometer/hygrometer, mister and shallow water dish.

Housing

Provide at least a 24 × 18 in (61 × 46 cm) footprint for one large adult, with adequate height for 8-12 in (20-30 cm) of substrate and clearance above. More space is strongly preferred, especially for groups. Use a secure lid and reliable cross-ventilation.

Lighting and Temperature

Lighting & UVB

An indirect day/night light cycle and shaded hiding places are adequate. Specialized UVB is not an established requirement. Keep the habitat out of direct sun and away from bright, drying lamps.

Heating & Temperature

Aim for about 73-80°F (23-27°C) by day and approximately 70-75°F (21-24°C) at night. Provide a cooler refuge; avoid sustained readings below 68°F (20°C) or above 85°F (29°C).

If the room is cool, stabilize room temperature or use thermostatically controlled external side-wall heat. Never use an under-tank heater beneath burrowing substrate.

Humidity and Hydration

Humidity & Ventilation

Aim for around 75-85% humidity, but verify that the lower substrate is continually damp, loose and crumbly rather than dry or flooded. Allow a few slightly drier surface spots.

Add clean water at an enclosure edge when deeper soil begins drying. Improve airflow if there is constant condensation, sour smell, flooding or pervasive mold; do not completely dry the enclosure.

Water & Hydration

Offer a shallow, stable water dish and refresh it regularly. Moist soil is essential for hydration and safe molting. Clean, dechlorinated water is preferable.

Substrate and Enrichment

Substrate

Provide about 8-12 in (20-30 cm) or more for full-sized adults where practical. This substrate is the main food supply and provides protected underground molting chambers.

Use pesticide- and fertilizer-free organic topsoil or leaf mold mixed throughout with abundant decomposing deciduous hardwood leaves and soft white-rotted hardwood. Add a thick leaf-litter cap and replenish as consumed.

Avoid cedar/pine, aromatic or treated wood, fertilized potting soil, perlite, sand-heavy bedding and pure coco fiber as the main substrate. Coco coir retains moisture but is nutritionally insufficient alone.

Decor & Enrichment

Add low cork bark and stable pieces of rotted hardwood for cover and grazing. Avoid tall climbing structures and loose heavy rocks that could fall or trap the animal.

Diet and Supplementation

Food & Feeding

The staple diet is decomposing hardwood leaves, rotten wood and associated decomposer material available within the soil at all times, not fresh produce alone.

Offer small amounts of washed squash, zucchini, carrot, sweet potato, mushrooms, cucumber or leafy greens about 2-3 times per week. Fruit is an occasional treat; remove leftovers before they spoil.

Very small amounts of prepared millipede food, fish flakes or other protein-rich extras may be offered occasionally. Excess fruit and protein attract pests and increase mold.

Supplements

Optional cuttlebone or plain calcium carbonate can be offered separately. Exact dosage needs are not established. Routine reptile D3/multivitamin dusting is unnecessary.

Handling and Cohabitation

Handling & Behavior

Support the whole animal with clean hands close to a soft surface and allow it to walk voluntarily. Never force it to uncoil or dig it up to be handled. Falls can cause severe injury.

Defensive secretions may stain or irritate skin and eyes. Wash hands after contact and avoid touching the face or mouth.

Cohabitation

Compatible adults can be kept together with enough space, food, hiding sites and deep damp substrate. Mixed sexes may reproduce. Do not keep with predators or animals that may disturb molts.

Routine and Seasonal Care

Cleaning & Routine Maintenance

Check temperatures, substrate moisture and food regularly. Remove spoiled fresh food, replace decayed hardwood material gradually and spot-clean contaminated areas.

Do not strip the whole substrate during ordinary maintenance; it supports the animal's diet and can contain eggs or vulnerable molting animals.

Shedding, Molting & Seasonal Changes

Underground molts may keep a millipede out of sight for weeks. Never dig into a molt chamber or force an animal to surface. Maintain stable warmth and moisture and evaluate sustained weakness or unnatural behavior.

Reproduction

Adult males have modified legs (gonopods) near the seventh body ring. Mixed-sex groups may lay eggs in deep substrate. Juveniles are small and grow slowly; preserve moist edible substrate, fine escape-proof ventilation and undisturbed chambers.

Common Health Concerns

Dry substrate or overheating can cause dehydration and weakness. Recheck moisture at depth as well as temperature.

Falls, forced handling and disturbed molt chambers can cause serious injury or incomplete molts.

Contaminated soil, treated wood, sour food and stagnant wet conditions can contribute to decline. Seek an exotic veterinarian familiar with invertebrates if problems persist.
