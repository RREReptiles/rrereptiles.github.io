White-Lined Gecko / Skunk Gecko Care Guide

Gekko vittatus

Arboreal Intermediate Nocturnal

White-lined geckos are fast, vocal, strongly arboreal geckos best suited to display-focused keepers. Modern care should provide generous vertical space, dense climbing structure, a usable heat and humidity gradient, low-level UVB, and varied live invertebrate prey.

Evidence note: species-specific husbandry studies are limited, so this guide combines documented natural history with conservative modern standards for nocturnal arboreal geckos.

Enclosure Requirements

  • Use at least an 18 x 18 x 36 inch vertical enclosure for one adult; larger is preferred.
  • Fill the enclosure with cork tubes, broad branches, vines, ledges, foliage, and several tight daytime retreats.
  • Use secure latches and carefully close cable gaps because this species is extremely fast and can escape through small openings.

Temperature, Lighting and Humidity

  • Provide low-level UVB around UVI 0.5-1.0 at the highest exposed perch, with true shaded areas near UVI 0.
  • Warm basking surface 86-90 F; daytime air 76-82 F; cooler lower zone 72-78 F; nighttime 70-75 F.
  • Allow humidity to cycle from roughly 55-70% during the drier daytime period to about 75-90% after evening misting, then partially dry again.

Diet and Supplementation

  • Feed a varied rotation of live insects such as crickets, roaches, black soldier fly larvae, silkworms, and appropriately sized mealworms.
  • Juveniles can be fed daily or nearly daily; adults generally do well with feeding about every other day or 3-4 times weekly.
  • Gut-load feeders for 24-48 hours and dust with calcium; use a coherent multivitamin/D3 schedule matched to the UVB setup.

Handling and Behavioral Management

  • Treat white-lined geckos primarily as display animals; they can be very fast, defensive, vocal, and capable of a firm bite.
  • For transfers, a cork tube, deli cup, or small transport container is usually safer than hand-catching.
  • House individually as the safest default. Never keep two males together for routine pet housing or mix substantially different sizes.

Health Monitoring and Safety

  • Track weight, appetite, stool quality, shedding, and hydration, especially during quarantine and after acquisition.
  • Persistent condensation, soggy substrate, stale air, or heavy mold indicate too much moisture or too little ventilation.
  • Watch for retained shed on toes or tail tip, bite injuries, tail damage, unexplained weight loss, and prolonged hiding or food refusal.
