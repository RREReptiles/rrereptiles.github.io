Ivory Millipede Care Guide

Chicobolus spinigerus

Detritivore Burrowing Beginner

Ivory millipedes are hardy southeastern U.S. detritivores that spend much of their time beneath leaf litter or buried in the substrate. Their enclosure should be built around deep, moist, edible soil rich in decomposing hardwood leaves and rotten wood rather than treating substrate as simple bedding.

Substrate is food: Most of an ivory millipede's nutrition should come from decomposing hardwood leaves and white-rotted hardwood mixed throughout a deep, pesticide-free substrate. Fresh produce is supplemental.

Enclosure Requirements

  • A ventilated 10-gallon enclosure works well for a small group; floor area and substrate depth matter more than height.
  • Use a secure lid with cross-ventilation because ivory millipedes can climb cork, branches, and enclosure seams.
  • Provide at least 4-6 inches of edible substrate, ideally roughly as deep as the adult millipede is long.

Temperature & Humidity

  • Normal room temperatures around 70-80 F work well; avoid direct sunlight and overheating.
  • Keep the lower substrate consistently moist like a wrung-out sponge while allowing the upper leaf litter and surface to dry slightly between mistings.
  • Good airflow is essential. Sour smells, persistent waterlogging, or heavy mold indicate excessive moisture or inadequate ventilation.

Substrate & Food

  • Use organic fertilizer-free topsoil amended heavily with pesticide-free decomposing hardwood leaf litter and soft white-rotted hardwood.
  • Avoid cedar, pine, aromatic softwoods, treated wood, fertilized potting soil, pesticide-exposed debris, and plain coco fiber as the primary substrate.
  • Offer small portions of vegetables and occasional fruit as supplemental food and remove soft leftovers before they spoil.

Calcium, Decor & Maintenance

  • Keep a calcium source available, such as cuttlebone or calcium carbonate, or sprinkle a small amount over food and leaf litter.
  • Provide cork bark, rotten hardwood, moss, leaf litter, and sturdy branches for cover, grazing, and climbing.
  • Never dig up a buried millipede unless there is a true emergency; buried animals may be resting or molting and are vulnerable to injury.

Cohabitation, Breeding & Handling

  • Ivory millipedes are peaceful and can be kept in same-species groups when enough substrate, food, and floor area are available.
  • Established mixed-sex groups may breed; eggs and tiny juveniles develop within the substrate, so refresh old substrate gradually rather than replacing everything at once.
  • Handle gently over a table or floor. Defensive secretions are not venom but can stain or irritate skin and eyes; wash hands after handling.
