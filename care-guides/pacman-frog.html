Pacman Frog Care Guide

Ceratophrys spp.

Terrestrial Solitary Intermediate

Pacman frogs are ambush predators that spend much of their time partially buried, but inactivity should not be mistaken for a lack of environmental needs. Modern care should provide deep safe substrate, a usable thermal and moisture gradient, appropriate lighting, varied whole-prey nutrition, and enough room for normal movement and burrowing.

Modern-husbandry note: Pacman frogs are often kept under bare-minimum or outdated conditions. Our recommendations intentionally prioritize behavioral opportunity, controlled overhead heat, low-level UVB when safely provided, clean water, deep substrate, and careful nutrition rather than treating a small wet box as adequate permanent housing.

Enclosure Requirements

  • Adult enclosure: about 24 x 18 x 18 inches or larger, with enough floor area for the frog to fully reposition and choose among microclimates.
  • Provide several inches of loose, soil-like substrate deep enough for normal burrowing; keep heavy decor stable so it cannot collapse onto a buried frog.
  • House Pacman frogs individually. They are opportunistic predators and can seriously injure or consume tankmates.

Temperature, Lighting & UVB

  • Create a warm side in the upper 70s to low 80s F and a cooler retreat in the low-to-mid 70s; avoid prolonged overheating.
  • Use overhead heat controlled by a thermostat rather than relying on bottom heat, which can overheat or dry the substrate where the frog burrows.
  • Provide a normal day/night cycle. Low-level UVB can be beneficial when supplied as a gentle gradient with shade and full retreat options.

Humidity, Water & Substrate

  • Keep the lower substrate consistently damp but not swampy; allow the surface to vary from moist to slightly drier so the enclosure is not stagnant.
  • Use amphibian-safe water. A shallow, easy-entry water dish should be available and changed whenever soiled.
  • Avoid gravel, calcium sand, aromatic wood products, and other substrates that are unsafe to ingest or do not support burrowing.

Diet & Supplementation

  • Base the diet on varied appropriately sized invertebrates such as earthworms, roaches, crickets, silkworms, hornworms, and similar feeders.
  • Rodents and other very fatty vertebrate prey should not be a routine staple; overfeeding and obesity are common captive problems.
  • Use a high-quality amphibian calcium/vitamin strategy and pay attention to vitamin A as well as calcium and vitamin D3.

Handling, Maintenance & Health

  • Handle only when necessary; amphibian skin is permeable and easily affected by soaps, lotions, sanitizer, nicotine, and cleaning residues.
  • Spot-clean promptly, remove uneaten prey, refresh substrate as needed, and prevent sour, waterlogged conditions.
  • Watch for abnormal redness, edema, weak limbs, jaw deformity, persistent poor appetite, severe weight change, retained shed, prolapse, or abnormal breathing.

Red Rocks Exotic Reptiles care information is educational support only. If you notice illness, injury, breathing issues, neurological signs, significant weight loss, or prolonged appetite loss, contact an appropriately experienced veterinarian.
