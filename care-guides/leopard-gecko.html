Leopard Gecko Care Guide

Eublepharis macularius

Terrestrial Beginner-friendly Crepuscular

Leopard geckos are ground-dwelling insectivores from semi-arid South Asia. Modern husbandry should provide real floor space, overhead daytime heat, low-level UVB, secure hides, and opportunities to dig, climb, hunt, and choose among different microclimates.

Modern care note: the old 10-gallon tank, heat-mat-only, reptile-carpet setup is not our welfare standard. This guide emphasizes measured heat and UVB gradients, enrichment, appropriate loose substrate for healthy animals, and individual housing.

Enclosure Requirements

  • Use at least a 36 x 18 x 18 inch enclosure for one adult; larger floor plans are preferred.
  • Provide at least three secure hides: warm, cool, and humid, plus sheltered routes between them.
  • Juveniles can use a full-sized enclosure when food intake and waste are easy to monitor and the habitat has abundant cover.

Heating, Lighting and UVB

  • Use overhead halogen or incandescent heat as the primary daytime heat source, with a basking surface around 94-97°F.
  • Target roughly UVI 0.5-1.5 for normally pigmented animals; keep albino or hypopigmented morphs closer to 0.5-0.7 with abundant shade.
  • Provide a clear day/night cycle and complete darkness at night; avoid colored night bulbs and heat rocks.

Humidity and Substrate

  • Keep the main enclosure relatively dry and ventilated, generally around 30-50% relative humidity.
  • Maintain a humid hide around 70-80% and inspect toes, tail tip, and eyelids after sheds.
  • After quarantine, a packed semi-arid mix around 60% organic topsoil, 30% washed play sand, and 10% clay provides safe digging opportunities for healthy animals.

Diet and Supplementation

  • Feed a varied rotation of appropriately sized live insects such as roaches, crickets, black soldier fly larvae, silkworms, and mealworms.
  • Gut-load feeders for 24-48 hours and adjust feeding frequency to age, body condition, season, and reproductive status.
  • Use calcium plus a reptile multivitamin containing preformed vitamin A; measured UVB reduces reliance on dietary D3 alone.

Enrichment, Handling and Cohabitation

  • Add cork, slate, low stable climbing features, textured rocks, leaf litter, and multiple feeding locations to encourage normal exploration and hunting behavior.
  • Let a new gecko settle and feed consistently before routine handling; scoop from below and never restrain or lift by the tail.
  • House leopard geckos individually for routine pet care. Breeding introductions are temporary managed events, not a normal cohabitation recommendation.
