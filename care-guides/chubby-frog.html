Chubby Frog / Asian Painted Frog Care Guide

Kaloula pulchra

Terrestrial / Fossorial Easy to Intermediate Nocturnal

Chubby frogs are stout narrow-mouthed frogs from South and Southeast Asia. They spend much of their time buried or under leaf litter and emerge especially during warm, humid conditions to forage on small invertebrates.

Evidence note: this guide combines published Kaloula pulchra natural history, professional zoo amphibian husbandry, current veterinary guidance, and conservative modern UV-lighting principles rather than relying on older pet-store minimums.

Enclosure and Burrowing

  • Use at least a 24 x 18 inch footprint for one or two similarly sized adults; larger makes gradients easier to provide.
  • Provide 5-6 inches or more of soft, pesticide- and fertilizer-free soil-based substrate so the frogs can fully bury themselves.
  • Add leaf litter, multiple low hides, sturdy plants, and a secure ventilated lid; Kaloula can climb surprisingly well when seeking shelter.

Lighting, Heat and Humidity

  • Provide a 10-12 hour day/night cycle with complete darkness at night and plenty of deep shade.
  • Low-level UVB is recommended when a true gradient can be created; roughly UVI 0.5-1.0 at the most exposed usable surface with near-zero shaded retreats is a conservative target.
  • Keep most of the enclosure around 72-78°F with an optional small warm area around 80-82°F, and maintain roughly 60-80% humidity with a real moisture gradient rather than saturated soil.

Water and Substrate

  • Provide a shallow, easy-exit water dish large enough for the frog to sit in comfortably without being forced to swim.
  • Use dechlorinated tap water or another amphibian-safe mineral-containing water; chloramine requires a conditioner designed to neutralize it.
  • Keep the lower substrate damp and diggable but never muddy or foul-smelling; persistent condensation and soggy soil indicate too much moisture or too little airflow.

Diet and Supplementation

  • Feed a varied rotation of small cultured prey such as crickets, roaches, black soldier fly larvae, small earthworms, silkworms, and appropriately sized mealworms.
  • Adults usually do well with a moderate meal every 3-5 days; juveniles need smaller meals more frequently. Adjust to body condition rather than appetite alone.
  • Gut-load feeders and use a coherent calcium/multivitamin plan that includes a usable source of vitamin A; vitamin D3 strategy should match whether measured UVB is provided.

Handling, Cohabitation and Health

  • Routine handling should be avoided. Use clean damp transfer cups or powder-free gloves rinsed with amphibian-safe water for necessary moves.
  • Similarly sized animals can sometimes be housed together with ample space, duplicated hides and feeding areas, but single housing is easiest for monitoring health and appetite.
  • Watch for obesity, appetite changes, red or ulcerated skin, abnormal swelling, weak posture, poor prey capture, weight loss, or persistent lethargy and seek an amphibian-experienced veterinarian when needed.
