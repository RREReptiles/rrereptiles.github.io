Bumblebee Millipede Care Guide

Anadenobolus monilicornis

Beginner-FriendlyTerrestrial / BurrowingDetritivoreApprox. 3-5 Year Lifespan

Bumblebee millipedes are small Caribbean detritivores known for their yellow-and-black banding. Adults are typically about 2-3 inches long and are straightforward to keep when their enclosure is built around deep, biologically rich, edible substrate.

The substrate is both habitat and food. Fresh produce alone is not a complete diet; decaying hardwood leaves and soft rotten hardwood must remain available throughout the enclosure.

Enclosure Requirements

  • A well-ventilated 5-10 gallon enclosure provides ample room for a small group. Floor area and substrate depth matter more than open height.
  • Use a secure lid and fine ventilation because juveniles can pass through surprisingly small gaps.
  • Add cork bark, moss, hardwood leaves, and other low cover while leaving enough substrate depth for complete burial.

Temperature & Moisture

  • Normal warm room temperatures around 70-80°F work well; avoid sustained high heat and direct sun.
  • Keep the lower substrate consistently moist like a wrung-out sponge while allowing the uppermost surface to dry slightly between mistings.
  • Good cross-ventilation is important. The enclosure should be humid and moist without becoming stagnant, flooded, or sour.

Substrate & Primary Diet

  • Provide at least 3-4 inches of pesticide-free organic soil mixed heavily with decaying hardwood leaf litter and soft white-rotted hardwood; deeper is beneficial.
  • Continually replenish hardwood leaves and rotten wood as they are consumed. Avoid cedar, pine, treated wood, fertilizers, pesticides, and plain coco fiber as the sole long-term substrate.
  • Do not dig up buried millipedes. They molt underground and are extremely vulnerable while soft.

Supplemental Food & Calcium

  • Offer small amounts of vegetables and occasional fruit once or twice weekly, removing uneaten fresh food before it spoils.
  • A small amount of protein-rich food can be offered occasionally, but supplemental foods should never replace the decomposing plant material in the substrate.
  • Keep a calcium source such as cuttlebone or plain calcium carbonate available.

Handling, Groups & Health

  • Bumblebee millipedes are peaceful and can be maintained communally when space and food are adequate; mixed-sex groups may reproduce readily.
  • Handle only briefly and close to a surface. Falls can injure them, and defensive secretions may irritate or stain skin, so wash hands after contact.
  • Persistent surface wandering, prolonged dryness, foul substrate, repeated failed molts, injuries, or unusual lethargy warrant a review of moisture, substrate quality, ventilation, temperature, and husbandry.
